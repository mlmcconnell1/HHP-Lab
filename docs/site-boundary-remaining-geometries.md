## Status definitions and current result

Three statuses are kept separate because a polygon comparison cannot establish the reporting jurisdiction:

- **Compatible**: evidence supports the same named administrative count unit for the stated scope. For the four cases reviewed below, that is a moderate-confidence recommendation for 2010–2020 and 2022–2024 as whole county or city-county outcomes. It does not mean identical annual HUD polygons or service-level allocations.
- **Incompatible**: affirmative evidence establishes a different scope for the relevant dimension. Every evaluated FY2023 polygon is technically non-identical to frozen B2024 geometry; this is an exact-polygon incompatibility only, not by itself proof of a different PIT/HIC jurisdiction. CT-503 has documented municipality-level CAN assignment differences before the January 2024 count, but its formal HUD count-jurisdiction status remains unknown.
- **Unknown**: the required year-specific count jurisdiction or complete geocode assignment is not evidenced. This is the reporting-scope status for the current panel, including every member not individually resolved below.

HUD describes its annual products as geographic boundaries and funding-status information for locally developed CoC areas ([HUD CoC GIS data catalog](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/continuum-of-care-coc-gis-tools), [HUD CoC Grantee Areas](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/continuum-of-care-coc-grantee-areas)). The Census Bureau documents that legally defined geographies can be updated annually from several programs ([Census 2023 geographic boundaries](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/geography-acs/geography-boundaries-by-year/2023.html)). A difference between annual CoC GIS files is therefore a signal requiring attribution; geometry alone cannot establish shoreline digitization, an approved CoC reporting change, or PIT/HIC count scope.

## Recommendation

For these four cases, use the **stable administrative count unit** for 2010–2020 and 2022–2024, with 2021 omitted by design. The reviewed primary records consistently identify the same whole county or consolidated city-county unit at the early and recent endpoints, and no merger, split, or transfer was found. No reporting-jurisdiction change was found in the evidence reviewed. This is a moderate-confidence recommendation for a panel keyed to the stable administrative count unit. It does not assert that all annual HUD polygons or exact selected-geocode sets are identical to the frozen 2024 polygons, or that each PIT/HIC operational allocation was recovered.

The 2023 HUD GeoCodes/PPRN report’s search-indexed rows provide CoC-to-geocode evidence for all four cases: TX-603 has El Paso city and El Paso County codes; HI-501 has the Honolulu code; CA-613 has El Centro and Imperial County codes; CA-501 has the San Francisco code. The report PDF URL is currently not directly retrievable (404), so these specific indexed rows were not archived as a parsed source.
